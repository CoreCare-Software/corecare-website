# Personal data breach procedure

Emergency contact: `security@corecaresystems.co.uk`  
Privacy contact: `privacy@corecaresystems.co.uk`

## First response

1. Protect people first. Stop ongoing disclosure, revoke affected sessions or credentials, isolate the route or service, and preserve relevant evidence.
2. Record the time detected and the time CoreCare became aware that a personal data breach may have occurred. Open a central breach case even if it later proves non-reportable.
3. Do not include passwords, full health records, payment-card data or unnecessary personal data in chat, email or observability logs.
4. Assign an incident owner and an independent decision reviewer. Identify each affected customer and whether CoreCare is controller or processor for each dataset.

## Assessment

Record the incident timeline, affected products and environments, data categories, special-category or criminal-offence data, approximate people and records, confidentiality/integrity/availability impact, ease of identification, vulnerable people, likely consequences, containment and residual risk. Preserve the reasoning, not just a low/medium/high label.

## Notification decisions

- When CoreCare is processor, notify the relevant controller without undue delay and provide information in phases as facts develop. The controller makes its regulatory decision, with CoreCare assistance.
- When CoreCare is controller, report a breach likely to risk people’s rights and freedoms to the ICO without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness. If later, explain the delay.
- Inform affected people without undue delay where the breach is likely to create a high risk, using clear language and practical protective steps, unless a lawful exception applies.
- Document every decision, including why ICO or individual notification was not required.

## Recovery and closure

Validate the fix, check for persistence or secondary exposure, restore safely, monitor for recurrence and communicate material changes. Closure requires an approved root cause, evidence list, customer/regulator communications, corrective actions with owners and dates, and a lessons-learned review. Retain the breach register entry under the published schedule and apply a legal hold where needed.
